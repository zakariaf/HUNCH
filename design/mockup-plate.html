    HUNCH  /  art directionDirection BPLATE

    The Loom is not glowing at you. You are drawing it.

    Direction A renders an artefact that lights up when you feed it. PLATE draws the other conclusion from the same premise: the machine is older than you and indifferent, so the only available response is to observe it carefully and write it down — which is exactly what a probe is. The surface is a plate in a field notebook. Being right is felt as pressure, never as light.

    beat 0 · t 0 ms
    What to watch

    Your declaration is the ink plate. The hidden law is the copper plate, and it prints 8–24 pt out of register — the size of the miss is how wrong you were. At beat 4 the platen closes: the four crosses close their 2 pt break, the platemark debosses, every stroke gains 0.5 pt for 40 ms. Nothing on this stage gets brighter at any point. The screen becomes heavier, not lighter.

    This is the whole bet, and it is worth stating as a risk on the first screen rather than the last: registration only pays off for a player who noticed the crosses were broken for the past twelve minutes. A bloom needs no prior. See Cost 4.

    PL. I

    The glyph system

    4 × 4 × 4 × 4  =  256, forever

    Four attributes, each occupying a register of the drawing that no other attribute may enter: the interior, the silhouette, the contour, and the index stroke below the body. Rows run in canonical order — fill → shape → pips → hue — because that order is fixed everywhere in the game, from VoiceOver to serialisation, and a pitch that reorders it teaches the wrong habit on page one.

    Judge this

    Whether the four channels are separable at a glance, and whether the rank order inside each one is obvious without being told. Rank is not decoration here: six of the eight law families compare ranks, so a player who cannot see that square outranks triangle cannot play band 4 upward.

    Thirty-two of the deck, at 64 pt

    All four channels survive with colour removed
    The panel that decides it

    The same thirty-two marks, same generator, same geometry — the lower block only has grayscale(1) laid over it. Nothing was redrawn and nothing was compensated. If you can still read four channels in the lower block, colour is a convenience in this game rather than a dependency, and every accessibility question after this one gets cheaper. The sample is (i × 39) mod 256, and it is deliberately the same thirty-two ids the other direction's greyscale panel uses, so that what you are comparing is two renderings and not two samples.

    The controlled pairs, colour removed

    Check the hue row specifically

    A stride-32 sample contains no pair of glyphs differing in one channel, so the block above cannot actually be used to test a single-channel claim — these four ladders can, because each one holds three channels fixed and walks the fourth. Look at the bottom row. amber and teal land on greys of 42.0 and 39.8 out of 255 — 1.05 ΔL* apart, comfortably inside a patch just-noticeable difference of roughly 2.3, so for reading purposes they are the same grey. They are told apart by 0° against 45°, and by nothing else. (What makes them near-identical is that grayscale(1) is a luminance matrix applied in gamma-encoded sRGB, not the fact that their WCAG ratios are both about 11.25 : 1 — equal contrast ratios against a shared ground would not on their own make two inks the same grey.)

    Found while building this renderer. §13.5 gives the body centre as +0.10·S and the index at −0.43·S in a y-up reading, but specifies the triangle apex at −90° and the N pip at −90° in a y-down reading. Taken literally in either single convention the glyph draws index-above-body or triangle apex-down. Resolved here as screen space, y down, body −0.10·S, index +0.43·S. Second: the index centre plus half-length reaches 0.5665·S, so frost bleeds 6.65 % of S past the bottom of its box — 2.9 pt at S = 44. Either the tile reserves a 1 : 1.134 box or the index register moves to 0.36·S. Both defects affect either direction.

    PL. II

    The PROBE surface

    375 × 667 pt · 1:1 Live

    Every region sits at the y-coordinate the layout solve already fixed. The phone is reproduced at true scale and is not enlarged, because the only useful question about a 44 pt key is how big 44 pt actually is in your hand. This one works. Tap the Dial to build a glyph, then tap the leading key to feed it to the Loom — the hidden law really is pips(cur) > pips(prev) AND shape ∈ {triangle, hexagon}, the verdict is computed and not staged, the ribbon remembers, and the tally crosses par at twenty-three.

    Judge this

    First, the verdict beat, which is the thing this direction has to win on: probe a few times and watch what happens at the throat. Nothing brightens. Ever. Admit is a closed copper ring that expands; reject is the same ring contracting and coming apart into four arcs under a doubled cancel. Second, reach: the Dial's cells are 70 × 48 pt and everything you touch sits below y 240, inside the right-thumb arc. Third, what is absent: there is not one numeral or letterform on this screen. The probe tally is a graduated printer's scale — 0.5 pt minor ticks at 6 pt pitch, a major tick every fifth — with par marked by a copper caret and the cap by a doubled ink tick. Fourth, the Dial: each cell is a picture of its own attribute, which is why the same widget is re-used on the Bench without a second lesson.

    Watch the sixth tile in the ribbon

    It is a twin — the same glyph as the fifth, probed twice — and it came back rejected where the fifth was admitted. Nothing about the glyph changed; prev moved. That is the whole of contextuality, stated by the transcript rather than by a tutorial, and it is why the Bridge's ghost toggle needs no explanation when it appears on the Bench two plates later. Every verdict in that ribbon is computed from the law, not typed in, and a shipped assertion re-derives all seven at load.

    PL. III

    The verdict, without luminance

    admit · reject

    This is where the two directions separate hardest. A dark ground can make a verdict out of brightness. Paper cannot: brightening an already-bright surface is invisible. So the verdict is spent on six achromatic channels at once — radius, closure, dash, weight, cancel, and whether the copper plate takes at all.

    Judge this — before you open the key

    Neither mark is labelled, and the two glyphs are identical: same fill, same shape, same pips, same hue, drawn by one call with one argument list. Only the verdict mark differs, so whatever you perceive is attributable to the verdict and to nothing else. Decide from the drawing alone which one the Loom accepted, then open the key below.

    The same pair with colour removed

    Judge this

    The verdict has to survive here too, and this is where a direction that spends brightness on it loses. Two deliberate departures from the comparison spec, both arguable and both yours to overrule: PLATE's admit does not bloom. Where Direction A puts 6 pt of outward glow, this puts 0.33 pt of hard-edged ink spread and an 8° overlap where the arc crosses its own start — a closed ring that visibly closed. And PLATE's reject holds its weight at 3 pt rather than collapsing to a 1.5 pt hairline, because on paper a thinner line reads as a weaker signal, not a colder one; the ring is taken apart instead. Presence against absence of the copper plate is additionally a 19.8× raw luminance difference between the two rings — WCAG 3.11 : 1 — against 1.78× / WCAG 1.68 : 1 for §13.7.2's brass-versus-cold ring pair. Note the two units. A raw Y ratio is written × on this page and a WCAG contrast ratio is written : 1, and they are never interchangeable: 19.8× and 3.11 : 1 are the same measurement of the same two inks.

    How the same verdict survives at 44 pt in the ribbon

    What changed here, and the risk that is left

    The rejected tile used to be a pure blind emboss — a stroke.hairline ring and a 0.5 pt highlight, an impression with no ink in it at all. It measured 1.43 : 1 on the ground.sunken the tiles are actually drawn over, and the token sheet three plates down says stroke.hairline is never state-bearing. The sheet won: the ring is now stroke.primary at 14.19 : 1, still dashed 4 / 3, and the emboss is kept as a third, additive cue rather than the whole signal. The achromatic argument is untouched — solid against dashed, copper against ink, tick present against tick absent, three channels before the emboss is counted. The risk that remains is Cost 7's: at 44 pt the admit and reject rings share a radius, so the two are told apart by dash pattern and ink rather than by size, and dash patterns are the first thing a low-DPI panel eats. That is the detail to put in front of a usability test first.

    PL. IV

    The Bench

    two rails · one coupler · no text Live

    The declaration surface, holding the canonical band-5 law: pips(cur) > pips(prev) AND shape ∈ {triangle, hexagon}. A Bridge on the leading rail with its trailing socket ghosted, a Ramp on the second, a welded bar between them for AND, and the live extension in the trailing column. Press the Seal. This is the money shot on the actual play surface at 1:1, not a diagram of it — see below.

    Press the Seal, then watch four things

    One: the two rule-tiles leave their rails and gather into a centred stack — the ink plate — arriving 8 pt out of register, with no overshoot, because a press does not bounce. Two: the hidden law prints behind it in copper at 55 %, off register, and the size of the miss is how wrong you were. Three, at beat 4: the copper slides to zero, the four registration crosses close their 2 pt break, the platemark debosses, and every stroke on screen gains 0.5 pt for 40 ms. Four, and this is the whole bet: check that nothing got brighter. The screen became heavier. If that reads as an event to you, PLATE works; if it reads as “things moved slightly”, Cost 6 is real and you should take it seriously.

    Judge this

    Whether a law is legible as a drawing. Four specific things: the wedge opens toward the larger side, and since the leading socket is always cur, an opening that faces leading is what > looks like — it is derived from the comparator token rather than typed, and a shipped assertion cross-checks it against the Assay's lit count, because mirrored it silently means < and lights 32 cells instead of 64. The ghost socket — dashed frame and backward chevron — is the entire contextual grammar in one toggle, and it was introduced diegetically in the ribbon ten probes earlier. The unlit Ramp cells are re-inked to stroke.secondary and struck with a diagonal cancel hatch, rather than dropped to 30 % opacity: on paper a 30 % tint of a 15.15 : 1 ink measures 1.78 : 1, which is one sub-threshold channel where the spec asks for two. And the Assay at 64 pt is 64 of 256 cells lit, stippled as 1.6 pt dots on a 4 pt grid — literally the same drawing, at one shared dot diameter, that becomes the Codex thumbnail on the next plate.

    A conflict in the source, resolved rather than overflowed

    §4.2 specifies “rails, 291 pt wide | THE ASSAY, 64 pt trailing column”, and §13.3 fixes a 16 pt outer margin and a 343 pt content column. 291 + 64 = 355, so those two sentences cannot both be true. Resolved here toward §13.3, because the margin is the harder constraint and the alternative put the Assay's trailing edge at x 375 — flush with the device edge, under the curved corner, which is the one place a state-bearing element must never sit. The rails take 273, the house 6 pt gutter follows, the Assay keeps its canonical 64, and the row closes at x 359. The Ramp's four 56 × 44 pt cells are held exactly; what gives is the inter-cell gutter, which §4.2 never specified. This needs a decision, not a patch — it is flagged here rather than absorbed silently.

    The Seal says “not yet” without words

    Judge this

    A 4 pt machined bar lies across the Seal while any rail is inert, any socket unbound, or the draft admits everything or nothing. Pressing it pulses the offending rail and does nothing else: no error text, no error state, no modal. The machine simply is not ready. In PLATE the bar is ink and texture, not colour — which is the direction's tightest constraint, since reject, strike, barred and counterexample must all be told apart by shape in one register. See Cost 5.

    PL. V

    The Codex shelf

    band 5 · 15 of 6,934

    A page is a law, identified by its extension, and its thumbnail is that extension drawn: the 16 × 16 deck in glyphID order, admitted cells as 1.6 pt ink dots on a 3.5 pt grid, rejected cells as bare paper. Every constellation below is computed from a real predicate, not sampled noise — which is why they have structure rather than texture. The dot diameter is one shared constant across the app, so the Assay on the previous plate and these thumbnails are the same drawing at two pitches rather than two drawings that resemble each other.

    Judge this

    Whether the wall reads. Two thumbnails cannot collide, because the extension is the identity, and canonical-key adjacency puts near-neighbours in extension space side by side, so a filling shelf develops texture you can navigate by. This is PLATE's strongest single argument: the constellation metaphor is literal on paper and merely poetic on glass. Cell 7 carries a fracture — a ragged foul-bite notch on the trailing-top corner, seeded from the plate number so a page's damage is stable forever. Cell 11 carries the doubled anomaly rim. Both are achromatic, and both survive at 44 pt.

    PL. VI

    Where text actually lives

    chrome only

    The play surface has no letterforms at all, so the entire typographic system has to carry Settings, the Codex chrome and the statistics screen — and nothing else. Two deltas from the shipped spec: section and micro gain 0.02 em of tracking, because letterspacing reads as engraving. numeral is untouched, because monospaced digits are not negotiable.

    Judge this

    Whether an instrument panel can be built out of a system face and still have a voice. Look at the destructive row: PLATE has no second accent to spend on danger, so a destructive action is set in ink over a foul bite — a ragged, torn rule. It is a texture, not a hue, and it costs nothing from the colour budget. Look also at the Codex instrument strip: seventeen probes against a par of twenty-three, drawn as a scale and written as a monospaced numeral, because this is the one place in the app where a number is allowed to be a number.

    PL. VII

    Three themes

    paper · board · increase contrast

    The same three objects — one glyph, one verdict ring, one ramp — in all three themes. High Contrast is white, not black: iOS Increase Contrast raises contrast, it does not invert polarity, and a light-first direction whose high-contrast theme flipped to black would ship a third artefact to the users least able to absorb a surprise.

    Judge this

    High Contrast implements all five of §13.11's deltas here, not the two that are easy: all four hues collapse to stroke.primary; the index stroke goes 0.273·S → 0.409·S (12 pt to 18 pt at a ribbon tile); every stroke weight steps +0.5 pt; the unlit ramp cell's ink steps up; and its diagonal cancel hatch doubles 1.0 → 2.0 pt. Compare the third pane's ramp against the first two — the hatch is visibly heavier, which is the delta that is easiest to claim and hardest to notice missing. The mark loses a channel's redundant copy and keeps the channel. Note the honest cost visible here: the dark theme is chalk on prepared board, a different object with a different register, and it is the weaker palette on the numbers that hold — ink-against-hue band separation 1.82× where light's is 4.09×. See Cost 5.

    PL. VIII

    The token sheet

    no literal hex in view code

    Contrast is WCAG 2.1 relative luminance against that theme's ground. The four hues are Okabe–Ito scaled in linear RGB by a per-hue factor k: linear scaling multiplies Y exactly and leaves CIE (x, y) unchanged, so every hue keeps its chromaticity and its position on every dichromat confusion line. Only lightness moves.

    The number this direction is bought on

    The shipped spec claims the fill ladder 0 → 22.7 → 38.6 → 100 % holds its rank order at every size. It does not hold it across hues. On the shipping dark ground a striped teal patch is only 0.8 ΔL* lighter than a dotted amber patch — L* 38.0 against 37.3 — which is well inside a patch just-noticeable difference of roughly 2.3. The rank order technically survives and is nonetheless unreadable: a higher fill rank in teal and a lower one in amber produce the same patch. PLATE's light palette carries that same worst-case margin at 5.5 ΔL*, a 6.9× improvement, because it is built the way ink behaves rather than the way a screen does.

    Corrected here. Earlier drafts of this plate named the wrong two patches — “a dotted teal patch is 0.8 ΔL* lighter than a striped amber patch”. Recomputed, dotted teal is L* 29.6 and striped amber L* 47.3, so that reading is wrong by 17.7 ΔL* and in the opposite direction; the 0.8 belongs to striped teal against dotted amber. The table's twelve margins were recomputed with it: eleven reproduce, and the shipping-light row moves from 4.1 / 1.3 / 10.8 to 4.5 / 1.7 / 12.9.

    Register segregation, as three luminance bands

    Why bands and not pairs

    The shipped spec checks register segregation pairwise and gets 1.36 and 1.41. PLATE makes it structural and checkable in one assertion: every ink belongs to exactly one band, bands never overlap, and a shipped test asserts each token's Y lands in its declared band. Minimum inter-band separation, as raw Y ratios and written × rather than : 1: hue against copper 1.35× light and 1.48× dark; ink against hue 4.09× light but only 1.82× dark. These are luminance-band separations for a segregation rule, not legibility figures — the corresponding WCAG contrasts are 1.23 : 1 and 1.43 : 1, which is fine, because two inks in different registers are never asked to be legible against each other. The dark theme's 1.82× is the weakest number in this direction and it is the arithmetic behind Cost 5.

    Duration and easing — no springs on the play surface

    What this buys at runtime

    The shader has no time term at all, so Reduce Motion is free and there is nothing to freeze. Ink squash replaces bloom, and squash is two draws into one layer with hard edges — no blur filter, no offscreen pass, ever. Where the shipped spec spends three offscreen blur layers per frame on the throat, the ribbon and the tail, PLATE spends zero, and the Assay's bloom exclusion stops being a special case.

    PL. IX

    What it costs

    verbatim, unedited

    Reproduced without softening from the direction document. A pitch that hides its costs is worthless for a decision, and item 1 is a disqualifier that no amount of the rest can answer.

      Those eight checks run at load, in this page, against the same code that draws the specimens above — they are not a screenshot of a test run elsewhere. They exist because the Bench states one law in three media at once (a wedge, a predicate and a sentence) and there is no reason those three should stay in agreement on their own. If any line reads ×, the plate above it is lying and the banner says so rather than the page quietly rendering the wrong drawing.

      Every glyph, ramp, ring, constellation, tick scale and tile on this page is generated at load by one function, glyph(fill, shape, pips, hue, size) and the geometry helpers around it, driven by the direction's own numbers. There is no hand-drawn path data and no image asset. System faces only, which is honest: the app ships no font bytes.

      What is held fixed so the two directions can be compared at all. Everything on this page is band 5, which §5.7 fixes at par 23 and cap 37: the instrument bar is a 37-graduation scale with the copper caret at 23, six taken in PROBE and seventeen on the Codex strip. The law is one law, pips(cur) > pips(prev) AND shape ∈ {triangle, hexagon}. The greyscale panel samples (i × 39) mod 256. Those three — one band, one law, one sample — have to be identical on both pitches or the client is reading a difference in the exhibit as a difference in the direction. Where the other direction differs on any of them, it is that page that should move, because these are the shipped constants.

      This page obeys the direction it is pitching — two inks and no third. There is no red, no green-for-good, no semantic colour anywhere on it. Damage is a texture. Page chrome follows your light or dark preference; the specimens do not, because the committed world is the thing being judged.
