The criterion, before anything else

The brief carries one instruction about this choice, and it is worth putting at the top rather than in a footnote: “Offer light and dark themes, but design dark-first — that's the real one.” PHOSPHOR complies with that instruction. Direction B, PLATE, overrules it explicitly and says so in its own first cost. Neither page is entitled to decide whether the mandate is negotiable — that call belongs to whoever owns the brief, and it should be made before the eight exhibits are read, not after. If the mandate holds, this comparison is a formality and PLATE's other arguments are notes for a later revision. If it does not, the two directions should be judged on the exhibits, and PLATE's attacks on the fill ladder and on frame cost are live — costs 7 and 8 below answer both.

Eight exhibits follow. Both directions render the same eight, in the same order, so you can lay them side by side. They are not yet drawn at a contracted scale — three of the eight differ in specimen size or sample between the two pages, so every specimen here prints the size it was drawn at rather than asking you to remember one. The phones are 375 × 667 — true scale, not scaled up, because a 44 pt target has to be judged at the size a thumb will meet it.

Exhibit 1 The glyph system

Four channels, four ranks each, and no channel that needs colour

Each row below is one channel walked from rank 1 to rank 4. Judge the ladders: does each one read as an order and not just a set? Fill should look like ink accumulating. Shape should look like corners accumulating. Pips should be countable without counting. The index stroke should sweep 135°, not cycle.

What to judge. Every mark here comes from the same generator that draws the game, at the geometry in the spec — body radius 0.37 S, index register at 0.43 S below centre, pitch floored at 5 pt, rungs drawn at S = 56. Nothing is hand-drawn. If a ladder reads wrong here, it reads wrong in the app. One geometry fix is visible in the pips rung. On circle and hexagon the S node lands at y = 0.27 S while the index register starts at 0.2935 S — 1.03 pt apart at S = 44, so at three and four pips the node and the top of a 90° index stroke overlap by about 3 pt and used to weld into one blob. The registers are structurally adjacent at every size, so no repositioning inside canon's numbers can separate them; instead the index stroke is now drawn before the pips, and §13.5's own 1 pt ground knockout ring — whose stated job is to separate a node “from the body stroke and from fill texture reaching the contour” — cuts the gap. The node stays countable and the tick stays a tick.

The one measurement Exhibit 1 was asserting without taking

The ladders above pin hue to amber, which is the flattering case. Below is the fill ladder's patch lightness L* on ground #0B0A08 for all four hues, computed in the page from the shipped hexes and the spec's coverage figures — not quoted. Read down a column and the ladder is clean. Read across and it is not.

32 of the 256, in colour S = 64
The same 32, colour removed grayscale(1)

This panel is the one to spend time on, and the sample is built so that it can actually be checked. The 32 are laid out four to a row. The first four rows each vary exactly one channel and hold the other three fixed — row 1 is hue alone (a striped triangle with two pips, walked 0° → 45° → 90° → 135°), row 2 is fill alone, row 3 is shape alone, row 4 is pips alone. Those are the four controlled ladders, and they are here in greyscale, which is the only place the claim can be tested. Rows 5–8 are a spread across the rest of the deck. An earlier sample walked the deck at a stride of 39 and contained no pair anywhere differing in one channel, so it could not test three of the four.

The right half is the left half with every hue destroyed.

Exhibit 2 The PROBE play surface · 375 × 667

The whole game, at the size it will actually be held

This one is live. Tap the Dial to build a glyph, then tap the leading key to feed it to the Loom. The Loom in this specimen is running one law and will answer honestly; the seven tiles already on the ribbon are consistent with it. Nothing on this screen is text, in any language.

What to judge

Whether the top third reads as an instrument someone else built. The bar at y 20–64 is deliberately at the edge of visibility: a mode sigil and a tick row per probe, in stroke.secondary at 3.26 : 1. It is etched, not lit.

The tick row's state is a height step, not a tint. Probes taken are full 11 pt ticks, probes remaining are 4 pt stubs on the same baseline — both in stroke.secondary, so both clear the 3 : 1 graphical floor and the count is carried by length. It used to run taken ticks against stroke.hairline, which put the only running state readout on the play surface at a 2.03 : 1 state gap and made a hairline state-bearing — which the token sheet's own row forbids.

Whether the light is rationed

Count the bright things. There should be one 96 pt glyph in the throat, seven small ones on the ribbon, and one lit cell per Dial ramp. Everything else is a hairline. If a second thing competes with the throat, the direction has failed its own rule.

Whether the targets are big enough

Dial cells are 70 × 48, the three commit keys are 44 tall, and everything you can touch sits below y 240, inside a right thumb's arc. The Loom is in the unreachable top third on purpose — it is read, never touched.

The par crossing

Keep probing past thirteen. On the probe that fills the last tick, the row inverts to one solid rule and a dim row below begins emptying the other way. It is the only direction reversal in the instrument bar, and the only thing the machine marks that is not a verdict.

Exhibit 3 The verdict pair

Closed and growing, against broken and shrinking

These two are unlabelled on purpose. The claim is that you already know which is which, and that you would still know with the colour gone, the sound off and the phone in a pocket. This is where the two directions differ most, so read them slowly.

What to judge. The left ring stays closed and expands R → 1.35 R over 200 ms, weight 3 → 1, with a 6 px outward bloom. The right ring contracts 1.35 R → R over 160 ms, collapses to a 1.5 pt hairline, breaks into four arcs that separate 3 pt, and takes one diagonal cancel stroke — with a 130 ms ∓2 pt shudder that is a shudder, not a bounce. Colour is the third copy, after direction and closure. Below, the same pair with colour removed.

Still opposite. If you can tell these two apart, the verdict channel does not depend on colour vision, on a working speaker, or on the player having been told anything.

Exhibit 4 The Bench · 375 × 667

Stating a law without a single word

The player has two rails, a coupler between them, and a live 16 × 16 picture of what their half-built law currently admits. The draft on the Bench here is pips(cur) > pips(prev) AND shape ∈ {triangle, hexagon} — a Bridge on pips with the trailing socket ghosted, welded with AND to a Ramp on shape. The pinned prev is the ghost-framed tile at the end of the ribbon, which is also the glyph the throat is holding, and it carries pips = two. Against that pin the Assay lights — of 256. That number is read off the rendered grid at load, and the grid's predicate is derived from the wedge and the ramp drawn beside it rather than typed in a second time — the two cannot disagree. It is also, deliberately, the same law Direction B draws in the same slot.

The ghost

The trailing socket wears a dashed frame and a backward chevron. That is the entire contextual grammar — one toggle — and the player has already met that exact mark twice: on the seed glyph at round start, and on the trailing ribbon tile, every frame of every round. The symbol does the naming that words are forbidden from doing.

Three ways to be off, and a withdrawn token

An unlit ramp cell swaps its mark from stroke.primary to stroke.secondary, gains a diagonal cancel hatch, and drops its border to a hairline. Three non-colour channels, each measured at 3.18 : 1 or better against surface.cell, with a 4.4× gap between the lit mark and the unlit one. opacity.cellUnlit = 0.25 is withdrawn: 25 % of a 3.3 : 1 token over #100E0A resolves to 1.23 : 1, and the cancel hatch at 55 % to 1.77 : 1. Two channels neither of which clears 3 : 1 is not two channels. This is an amendment to §4.2 and it is the only one on the page that changes a canon value rather than adding one.

Where the 343 pt column breaks

The rails are 291 pt and the ramp cells 56 × 44, both exactly as §4.2 specifies, so this Bench and Direction B's are the same widget at the same size. But §4.2's own row — rails 291 | Assay, 64 pt trailing column — sums to 355, and §13.3's content column is 343 inside 16 pt margins. It does not fit, by 24 pt. Both directions resolve it the same way, by running the Assay column to the trailing edge at x 311–375. That is a real contradiction in the GDD, it is not ours to settle silently, and it is flagged here rather than hidden by quietly narrowing the rail to 267 — which is what this page used to do.

Barred, and ready

Below: the same Seal in both states, at S = 76. The machine says "not yet" by putting a 4 pt machined bar across the control. No error text, no modal, no red — the bar is a machine state, not a warning, and pressing it pulses the rail that is at fault and nothing else.

Exhibit 5 The Codex shelf

A wall of constellations

Every law the player has ever found becomes one 60 pt cell: the 16 × 16 deck grid with the cells that law admits lit. The thumbnail is the law's extension, not its spelling — so no two can collide, and a filling shelf acquires a texture you can genuinely read from arm's length.

What to judge. Whether fifteen of these on one shelf are distinguishable at a glance, and whether you would want two hundred. Two carry overlays: one has a 2 pt corner notch — a fracture, a page won after a wrong declaration — and one has the doubled rim of an Anomaly page. Both are geometry first, colour second. The two densest cells are contextual laws, whose thumbnails quantise to the same four-step ink ladder the fill channel already uses — and they now really do. The rungs are derived from {0, 22.7, 38.6, 100} % by taking the square root of the coverage to get a side length, which puts the insets at 0 · 0.2618 · 0.1894 · 0 of the cell. They were eyeballed at 0.36 and 0.20, which produced 7.8 % and 36 % — the dotted rung was a third of its stated weight and read as an almost-empty cell, so the claim of one shared ink ladder was false as rendered.

Exhibit 6 Chrome — where the words live

The only three screens with letterforms on them

Text is banned below the chrome bar, so all of it collects in Settings, the Codex, Statistics and About. That makes the typography load-bearing in a narrow place: it has to sound like an instrument panel and still be a comfortable iOS list.

Three Settings rows. The switch is drawn, not tinted — a 51 × 31 track and a 27 pt slug, ON = filled and trailing, OFF = hollow and leading. Two non-colour channels and no accent at all, because the accent budget is three elements per screen and a Settings list has eight toggles. The Theme control is the tightest text in the app: four segments inside 343 pt, and HIGH CONTRAST is what sets the size.

A Codex page's instrument strip. Every numeral is monospaced, always — a proportional digit that shifts a column on every probe is a bug, not a preference. The tick row is the player's best probe count against that band's par; the two brass marks are the Seal marks; the hairline across the rim is a fracture. This one page is the single element on the screen allowed to spend the brass.

Exhibit 7 The three themes

One sample, three worlds, no lost information

The same glyph, the same admit ring and the same ramp, resolved in Dark, Light and High Contrast. Dark is the design. Light keeps every piece of geometry and swaps the ground, carrying an ink keyline under every state-bearing register. High Contrast collapses all four hues to the foreground colour — and the game is still fully playable, because the hue channel was never the colour. The ramp shown is the hue ramp, which is the one that makes that last sentence checkable.

What to judge. Sample striped / hexagon / three / frost at S = 96. In High Contrast the index stroke lengthens from 0.273 S to 0.409 S — 12 pt to 18 pt at ribbon size — every stroke gains 0.5 pt, both bloom passes and the grain die, and unlit ramp cells carry their mark and hatch at full weight in stroke.secondary (9.68 : 1 on black) rather than being dimmed. One colour, four hues, no loss.

The light theme's hue channel, stated without the flattering version. Okabe–Ito is canon and verbatim in both themes, so on #F4EFE4 the raw hue ratios are amber 1.96 · teal 2.98 · frost 2.01 · rose 2.67 — every one below the 3 : 1 graphical floor. Canon's answer is a stroke.primary keyline, but it was drawn under the silhouette only, which is the one register that is not the hue channel. The index stroke is the hue channel; the pip discs are the pips channel; the interior texture is the fill channel. All three were sitting at 1.96–2.98. The keyline now runs under all four: the index at IW + 1, the pip knockout ring painted in ink rather than ground, and the fill texture given an ink core with the hue drawn inside it, so measured coverage stays exactly 0 / 22.7 / 38.6 / 100 % and the ladder is untouched. Every state-bearing edge in the light theme is now 15.6 : 1, with Okabe–Ito unre-lit. And note honestly what the light theme still is: the same machine, lit — a faithful translation and a thinner idea than the dark. Weakness 2 says so in the direction's own words. Its hexes stay provisional until something ships in it.

Exhibit 8 The token sheet

Every value, in all three themes, with its contrast against its own ground

Flat, named, exhaustive. Theme.token(_:) resolves against the active theme and there is no literal hex anywhere in view code. Ratios are WCAG 2.1 against that theme's own ground — and they are computed in this page from the hexes in the row beside them, every cell, on load. Where canon's published figure differs by more than a rounding step it is printed after the measured one in brackets, so you can see both rather than take either on trust.

† In the light theme every glyph carries a stroke.primary keyline beneath the hue — and, as of this page, beneath the index stroke, the pip discs and the fill texture too, so every state-bearing edge is 15.6 : 1 rather than only the silhouette. The raw hue ratios in the light column are the honest ones and they are all below 3 : 1; the keyline is what carries the channel, not the hue. ‡ text.secondary is new here. stroke.secondary's own Use cell says “chrome rules, tick marks”, and at 3.26 : 1 it is a graphical-object value, not a text value — but the in-app specimens were setting 10.5–15 px copy in it, which is a WCAG 1.4.3 failure at every one of those sizes. Text now resolves to text.secondary and stroke.secondary is confined to marks.

Costs Weaknesses — honest

Eight ways this direction can fail

The first six are quoted verbatim from the direction document, unedited. Costs 7 and 8 are new to this page and are marked as such: they are the two the document does not carry, they are both places Direction B attacks and PHOSPHOR had no answer, and one of them contradicts a sentence inside cost 1. A pitch that hides its costs is worthless for a decision, and a pitch that only lists the costs its own author thought of is not much better.

One note on this page, since you will notice it

Nothing on this page casts a shadow. No card, no plate, no phone frame, and nothing has a corner radius above 2 px. Surfaces separate the way they separate in the app — by a hairline and a small step in ground — because a pitch for a direction whose only depth cue is luminance should not be allowed to cheat with elevation. And in your light theme the page is not a dark room: it is a technical drawing, which is exactly what weakness 2 says PHOSPHOR becomes when you turn the lights on. The specimens never change. Only the room around them does.
