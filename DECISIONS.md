| 16 | **`HunchCore` gains a ninth source target, `Tokens`**, which `08 §1`'s tree does not list. | `DESIGN-SYSTEM-SCOPE.md` §4.4 rules the token layer into `HunchCore` so that `swift test` can assert every contrast ratio with no simulator; `package-manifests.md` §2 is the manifest of record and does list it. |
| 17 | **`Tokens` does not depend on `Glyphs`**, even though `Palette.Hue` mirrors `Glyph.Hue`'s four cases. | That mapping is one four-arm `switch` owned by the renderer. One switch is cheaper than a package dependency edge, and it keeps `Tokens` a leaf. |
| 18 | **The environment resolution order is Select → Scale → Offset → Derive.** `weight(.body)` under Bold Text *and* High Contrast is `3.0 × 1.25 + 0.5 = 4.25`, never `(3.0 + 0.5) × 1.25 = 4.375`. | The GDD states both modifiers (§13.11) and never their order. §13.11 spells Bold Text multiplicatively with worked values that must hold (`hairline` 0.5 → 0.625) and High Contrast as a flat `+0.5 pt`; an offset that also got multiplied would silently become `+0.625` and the two accessibility settings would stop composing independently. Asserted both ways, the second negatively. |
| 19 | **§13.11's "9.7 : 1 High Contrast floor" is asserted at the measured 9.68.** | The binding member of the state-bearing set, `stroke.secondary` on `#B0B0B0`, measures **9.683**, and `hunch-design-tokens` already documents it as 9.68. ## E05 — The rule grammar

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | **`StructuralFault.guardGateEqualsBranch` and `.guardBranchesEqual` are unreachable through `structuralFault`, and are kept anyway.** | §3.4 fixes the check order — depth, coupler-over-non-term, leaf count, term caps, per-attribute cap, duplicate leaves, *then* the per-production operand rules. A guard has three leaves, so `gate == branch` puts all three on one attribute and the per-attribute cap fires at n = 3 first; `then == otherwise` makes two identical leaves and the duplicate-leaf cap fires first. Both malformed guards are still rejected, by an earlier rule. The cases are kept because they state the BNF rule the generator builds against, and because reshuffling the order to reach them would change which fault every *other* malformed law reports — and the tests assert that order. Found by writing the test, not by reading. |
| 25 | **RNF's fourth sort field, pinned.** An atom's subset mask; for a relational or contextual the *trailing* attribute's canonical index; for a guard or aggregate a written-down ordinal over the payload. Never `hashValue`. | §3.4 names the key `(kindOrdinal, attrOrdinal, cmpOrdinal, subsetBitmask)` but the fourth is not total across productions. It must be deterministic *across processes* because the ordering is stored on disk — `hashValue` is seeded per process and would silently relay out every saved Bench draft. |
| 26 | **A same-attribute *Bridge* pair does not merge, even though its extension would.** `>` OR `==` on one attribute is `>=` as an extension, and RNF leaves it as two terms. | §3.4's merge applies to two `<atom>` operands only. Merging the Bridge case would be an *extension* fact, and §3.4's closing line says RNF is explicitly not the equivalence test — widening it would break G10's node-identity promise, which is what catches a renderer that transposes `cur` and `prev`. |
| 27 | **`SplitMix64` moved from `LawGeneration` to `Glyphs`.** | `Glyphs` is the universal leaf (`08 §1`: *"leaf; empty dependencies"*), and three things above it need the finaliser: `Laws.LawKey`, `Archive.Anomaly` (§11.6) and the generator. `LawGeneration` gains a dependency on `Laws` in E06, so importing it *from* `Laws` would be a cycle — and the only alternative, re-typing §11.6's constants in a second place, is precisely the failure that makes a globally shared daily law a coin flip. `LawGeneration`'s manifest rows are dropped until E06 writes its first file, per the target-ceiling rule. |
| 28 | **Attribute liveness permutes `cur` and `prev` INDEPENDENTLY.** | §3.6 says *"`T != permute_a(T)` for at least one non-identity permutation"* and does not say which positions it applies to. Permuting both halves of the pair index at once leaves `RANK pips(cur) == PREV RANK pips` invariant — equality survives any relabelling applied to both sides — so `pips` reads as dead, G6 rejects it, and §3.3's entry-level contextual law, the one that *"must exist"*, is silently deleted from bands 5 and 7. The correct reading is *dependence*, which is what "pivotal" means in G6's own wording. Frozen by `equalityContextualLawIsLive`. |
| 29 | **`Band.systemic.minLeaves` is 4, not the family minimum of 3.** | §5.2 publishes δ = 0.928 for the band-8 parity exemplar, and only `minLeaves = 4` reproduces it — 3 gives 0.9433, which is outside the 0.001 tolerance every other exemplar meets. The epic's reconstructed `minLeaves` table says 3; the published δ wins, because it is the design's own output. The consequence is coherent rather than a fudge: a 3-attribute `COUNT` clamps to zero as well, so `m1` is zero for *every* aggregate and leaf count carries no difficulty signal anywhere inside band 8 — which is exactly §5.2's argument that band 8 is hard by **symmetry**, not by size. Frozen by all eight exemplar assertions. |
| 30 | **`LawIndex.achievableDifficultyRange(for:)` is not `Band.difficultyRange`, and the serving policy must clamp into it.** | §5.1's modifiers are bounded above by 0.124, but several are *structurally zero for a whole family*: every band-1 law is a single atom, so `m1` is zero (`leafCount == minLeaves`) and `m2` is zero by definition — an atom's marginal deficit is zero, because varying that attribute is exactly what makes the lamp answer. Band 1 can therefore only reach roughly the bottom third of its nominal range. Serving `band.centre` made the generator burn all 200 attempts and fall back to the anchor **200 times out of 200**, which is silent because the anchor is a perfectly good law. The task file predicted this exact failure and named the missing member; this is it. |
| 31 | **The determinism test is a committed golden file, not an in-process repeat.** Regenerating it requires `HUNCH_RECORD_GOLDEN=1`, and the recording path deliberately **records an Issue** so a re-record can never be mistaken for a passing run. | In-process repeatability is necessary and not sufficient: a `Hasher`-seeded `Set`, an `ObjectIdentifier` ordering or a `Dictionary` iteration all reproduce inside one process and diverge across two. Only a file written by one process and read by another can fail on a per-process seed, which is exactly the class of bug the brief's "across processes" clause names. |

## E08 — The PROBE play surface

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | **`Round` is a `@MainActor @Observable final class`, and it is called `Round`.** Not `RoundState`, not `RoundViewModel`, not a value threaded through a call. | `04 A18`'s triggers 1 and 2 both fire, which is what makes it an *earned* screen-scoped observable rather than the reflex one: it is an eight-phase machine with locked-input windows, two strikes and a live draft, and that state is read by four sibling views — throat, Dial, ribbon, instrument bar — that must agree on it frame by frame. `A19`'s pass-through test passes: delete `Round` and the phase *timing*, the input lock and the exhaustion cadence break; the phase table, the ribbon, the verdicts and the score do not, because they are `HunchCore`'s and are tested there. The name is `N40`/`08 §3`'s bare domain noun — the round **is** the thing on the screen, and a `ViewModel` suffix would name the pattern instead of the domain. |
| 33 | **`RoundPhase.advance(_:on:)` returns `RoundPhase?`, and `nil` means the event was refused.** No trap, no `default:`, no fallback phase. | Every input lock in the game *is* a refused transition — `probing` and `declaring` are the only open-input phases (§6.1) — so a tap landing one frame into the 420 ms adjudication beat has to vanish, and it should vanish in exactly one place. Trapping would turn a legal race into a crash; returning the same phase would make "refused" and "self-transition" indistinguishable. Written as a switch over the phase with an exhaustive inner switch over the event, so adding a phase breaks the function and adding an event breaks all eight arms. |
| 34 | **Exhaustion is a flag set at commit and consumed at the end of the beat, not a phase change inside `commit`.** | §6.11 case 4 is explicit: the cap-th verdict is *still delivered in full*. Transitioning to `revealing(.exhausted)` inside the commit would swallow the last bit the player paid for — they spent a probe and would never see its verdict. The difference between "beat over, keep probing" and "beat over, round over" is invisible to the phase, so it has to be carried by the event: `.capReached` rather than `.beatCompleted`. |
| 35 | **`Duration.seconds` goes through integer nanoseconds.** | `Double(whole) + Double(atto)/1e18` rounds twice, and `.milliseconds(1_840).seconds` returned `1.8399999999999999` — one ulp below §13.7.1's reveal. Pre-existing since E04·T08 and invisible until `swift test --package-path Modules` ran on the host: the value is one ulp off, so nothing looked wrong, and a duration that is not the duration the design states is exactly the kind of drift the token layer exists to prevent. |
| 36 | **The Pro Max commit bar is 54 pt, not 60.** §6.2 gives the region as `y 868–922` and then calls the same row "60 pt tall". | The coordinates win: they are what the region table is *for*, and they are what the safe-area arithmetic closes against — `44 + 200 + 114 + 50 + 342 + 44 + 54` plus the three declared gaps is 860, which is exactly `922 − 62`. A 60 pt bar overflows the safe area by six points. 54 still clears §12.8's 44 pt target floor with the 4 pt inter-key gutter on top. Recorded because the sentence is still in canon and reads like a correction of the table. |
| 37 | **The compact Dial's 8 pt residual goes to the BOTTOM of the region.** §4.1's `4 × 60 + 3 × 8 = 264` leaves 8 pt inside a 272 pt region. | `dialRow(0).minY == dial.minY` puts the first ramp directly under the ribbon and makes the Dial's top edge exactly the point §6.2's reach paragraph measures. Splitting the residual, or putting it on top, moves the reach number by 4–8 pt against the 460 pt predicate for no gain — and the bottom of the Dial already abuts an 8 pt gutter, so the eye reads the residual as part of that gutter rather than as a gap. |
| 38 | **§6.2's three SE reach figures are each 20 pt short and are corrected in canon.** The Dial's top edge is 431 pt up, not 411; the throat 603, not 583; the ribbon 491, not 471. | All three subtract from 647 — the safe *height* — while the region table they subtract is in absolute screen coordinates, where the bottom safe edge is y = 667. The Pro Max figures use the absolute edge and are right, which is what makes the SE ones diagnosable rather than ambiguous. Every conclusion in the paragraph survives: 431 ≤ 460, and 603 and 491 are both above the band. Corrected rather than reproduced, because the layout asserts the numbers and a test cannot hold two readings. |
| 39 | **Every scalar in `PlaySurfaceLayout` is `CGFloat`, never `Double`** — including the pitches, which are otherwise token-shaped. | `#expect(rect.minY == someDouble)` compiles through the implicit conversion and then reports **failure**, and so does `#expect(rect.minY != someDouble)` — the macro is not comparing numerically at all, while the identical expression bound to a `let` first is `true`. It fails loudly rather than silently, so nothing can pass on a lie; but it makes the natural spelling of every geometry assertion in five epics unusable. One type across the layout removes the mixed comparison instead of asking everyone to remember it. |
| 40 | **`DeviceClass` is a predicate over the safe height, not a device list.** `large` requires its fixed rows plus a throat at least as tall as its 128 pt glyph — 804 pt of safe height. | A list of screen sizes is wrong the day a device ships. The predicate reproduces both of §6.2's tables exactly, sends an iPhone 16 (759 pt safe) to `compact` where its single ribbon lane belongs, and falls out with a pleasing check: the compact class's fixed rows plus its minimum throat come to **exactly 647**, the SE's whole safe height. The SE is the floor by construction, not by assertion. |
| 41 | **`Round.lastTouched` starts at `.fill`, not `nil`.** | The throat swipe is §6.3's cheapest controlled variation, and a gesture that does nothing until some *other* control has been used is a gesture nobody discovers — the player who tries it once on the first frame and gets nothing does not try it again. `.fill` is the canonical order's first (§2), so the default is the same rule the Dial, the Bench, the Codex and VoiceOver already follow rather than a new one. |
| 42 | **`GlyphRenderer.draw` takes a `registers:` subset, and hue's subset is all four.** | §6.3's "only the changed register animates" is unimplementable without it: the alternative is compositing two whole glyph canvases, and a glyph-bearing region gets exactly **one** bloom bed per frame, not two. Hue is the exception worth stating — it is the index stroke *and* the ink colour of every other pass, so a hue step moves no geometry and recolours the whole drawing. That still satisfies §6.3, whose actual claim is that the silhouette, contour nodes and index stroke *hold perfectly still*. |
| 43 | **The throat's glyph is clamped by the region, and the clamp is against `2 · 0.37 · S · 1.35`, not `1.35 · S`.** | The ring expands to a multiple of the **body radius**. Sizing the well against the box side would over-reserve by nearly three times and shrink the glyph on both devices for nothing. The compact class is the binding one — 112 pt of well against a 96 pt glyph — so above roughly AX1 the compact throat stops growing while the large one keeps going to the ceiling; §13.11 says the region does not grow, so something has to stop, and it is the glyph rather than the ring. |
| 44 | **A ribbon-load leaves `lastTouched` alone and sets `changedRegister` to `nil`.** | Two halves of one rule. Nothing was *touched*, so the throat swipe's target must not move under the player — a load would silently re-aim the gesture at whatever attribute the loaded glyph last differed in. And a wholesale adoption is not a controlled variation, so animating one register would be a lie about what happened: the throat crossfades the **whole** glyph on a load, and only on a load, which is the one legitimate use of that transition. |
| 45 | **The cancel hatch is drawn on `multi` and `stops` cells only, never on `single` or `exactlyOne`.** | On a draft composer an unlit cell means *not chosen*; on a rule tile it means *rejected*, and only the second is a claim about the law. The hatch is that claim drawn (§4.2 makes it the non-colour channel for exclusion), so hatching the Dial's three unselected cells would tell the player their draft excludes those values — on a surface where the draft excludes nothing, because it is one glyph. |
| 46 | **§12.8's Dial cell growth lands in the cell's HEIGHT; its width is capped by the row's budget.** §12.8 asks for 70 × 48 → 84 × 58; `44 + 4 × 84 + 3 × 6 = 398` does not fit a 375 pt screen. | No ramp anywhere in the design scrolls horizontally, and the 44 pt header *is* the target floor, so neither can give. The height can: §13.11 already sanctions the Dial's four ramps scrolling vertically inside a region that does not grow, so that is where the extra type size goes. The gutter tightens to 4 pt at accessibility1 and not before, which is what §12.8's table actually says — reading it as "any scale above 1" tightens the gutter a whole category early and buys width nobody asked for. |
| 47 | **`VerdictRing.State.twin` carries BOTH verdicts, not `admitted: Bool`.** | The case's own comment already claimed the split ring — *"when the two verdicts differ the ring draws SPLIT"* — and a single `Bool` cannot express it, so the drawing quietly could not do what the comment said. Both verdicts makes the split representable: the outer ring opens **180°**, half open and half closed, rather than taking the reject gap, because the contradiction is a shape rather than a degree. Same glyph, same reading, two answers — §6.6 layer 4's clearest wordless statement of contextuality, and `ribbon.md` §8 names losing it as a defect. |
| 48 | **The seed keeps the head of the ribbon under verdict sort.** | It has no verdict to sort by. Sorting it into the admit block would make the sorted ribbon claim the round *opened* with an admit, which is exactly the misreading the sort exists to prevent. The partition is stable, too, and deliberately not a comparator: any sort over the verdict alone would be free to reorder within a block, and the within-block chain order is the only thing that still lets a sorted ribbon be read as a history. |
| 49 | **`Modules/Sources/Feedback/` is created in E08, one phase before its owner epic, and holds only its value half.** `Cue`, `CuePlayer`, `SilentCuePlayer`, `RecordingCuePlayer` — no synthesiser, no haptic engine, no `VoiceBank`, no §13.8/§13.9 parameter tables. | A deliberate deviation from `01 P12`, and the reasoning is P12's own. §6.5 *is* an owner section for the cue **firing points**, so defining the vocabulary now means E20 attaches players to points that already exist and have already been timing-tested, instead of discovering them a phase late. What P12 protects against — inventing a boundary whose shape you have not learned — does not apply here, because `feedback-target.md` gives the shape in full. The target takes no default isolation: a cue vocabulary is data. |
| 50 | **The first tap inside the lock is QUEUED, not dropped, and T01's test was rewritten to say so.** | §6.5 is explicit — one tap during the lock is honoured at t = 420 ms and compresses the following travel to 180 ms; the *second* is dropped. T01 shipped before the gate existed and asserted the simpler rule, so it read "dropped" for a case the design honours. Rewritten rather than worked around: the round-level consequence is that the probe count never moves early, and the queue itself is `InputGate`'s to test. |
| 51 | **`landVerdict()` does not change the phase.** | The verdict *lands* at t = 260 — ring, cue, haptic and VoiceOver all on that frame — but input stays locked until t = 420. Reopening input when the verdict lands would let a player outrun the hold, and a hold that can be outrun is not a hold. Keeping the phase change in `endVerdictBeat()` alone is also what let three suites written before the beat existed keep passing unchanged. |
| 52 | **A twin-of-seed at probe 0 is an ordinary first probe: `isTwin` is false.** | §6.3 says probe 1 *defaults* to a twin of the seed; §6.11 case 2 says the seed glyph never gains a verdict ring. Both hold at once because the two sentences are about different things — the **default action** is a twin of the seed, while the **ribbon marking** needs two adjacent *probes*, and the seed is not a probe. `isTwin` is therefore decided by adjacency inside `Ribbon.probe`, never by which key was pressed, which also means a player who reaches the same glyph by editing the Dial gets the same marking as one who pressed twin. |
| 53 | **`Scoring.threeMarkFraction` is named in `HunchCore` and read by both callers.** | Two rules key off 0.6: §6.9's third Seal mark and §6.6's breath. Writing the literal in `LoomFeature` would give a locked §5.7 constant a second home, and the two would drift the first time the mark threshold is retuned — leaving a hint that fires at a point that no longer means anything. |
| 54 | **The breath latches on the press, not on the verdict, and never returns.** | Its job is to teach that the twin key exists, and pressing it has done that whatever the verdict was. Latching on the press also survives §6.11 case 5: a player who presses twin and immediately backgrounds the app does not find the hint waiting again on resume, which would read as the game not having noticed. |
